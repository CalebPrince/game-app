Thirty metres above the ground, the canopy walkway at Kakum National Park sways gently with every step. Seven narrow bridges connect the giant trees, and from them visitors see a world that most people never notice: hornbills gliding between branches, butterflies resting on sunlit leaves and monkeys calling from somewhere unseen. Yet the walkway was not built simply for thrills. Park officials hoped that if people experienced the forest from above, they would value it enough to protect it. Since the walkway opened, nearby communities have earned income from tourism instead of logging, and the forest has become a classroom for thousands of students each year.
